Policy Universes: optimal policy comparison

Published

October 29, 2026

Step 4 of specs/POLICY_UNIVERSES_OPTIMAL_POLICY_PLAN.md. Generated by node scripts/compare.js (20 s on this machine).

Approaches (all played in the true nonlinear engine, on the players’ own buttons):

  • No policy: no fiscal change, and the real rate held at its neutral 1% (the nominal rate follows expected inflation, in the players’ 25 basis point steps).
  • Taylor rule: Mankiw’s rule (0.5, 0.5), the game’s autopilot.
  • LQ rule: the infinite-horizon linear-quadratic optimal feedback rule (discount 0.95), rounded to the buttons, with its instrument costs tuned for each economy to the lowest welfare loss (optimal.js tunedCosts).
  • Search: finite-horizon perfect-foresight coordinate search over the buttons, started from no change, the no-policy path, the Taylor path and the LQ path.

Scoring: total welfare loss \(\sum [w_u(u-5)^2 + w_\pi(\pi-2)^2]\) over the game plus 4 tail rounds with no new policy. Share of the achievable gain = \((L_\text{none} - L)/(L_\text{none} - L_\text{search})\): 100% matches the search, 0% is no better than doing nothing. Shares are reported only where the achievable gain is above 0.5 loss points (180 of 180 cases).

Cases: 5 presets x 4 events x 3 game lengths (8, 12, 20) x 3 welfare weightings = 180.

Findings

Revised 2026-10-28 after two changes to what is compared (review points 3 and 4):

  • No policy now means no fiscal change and the real rate held at its neutral 1%: the nominal rate follows expected inflation, moved in the players’ own 25 basis point steps and phase-in. Before, it meant the nominal rate held at 3%, which with expectations that follow inflation is itself a destabilizing policy. Its path is also one of the search’s starting plans, so the optimal path is never worse than no policy.
  • The LQ rule’s instrument costs are tuned, not assumed: for each economy and pair of welfare weights, the pair of costs (from a grid) whose rule gives the lowest true welfare loss over the four events, relative to no policy. Before, they were fixed at 0.05 and 0.8.

  1. The LQ rule is still close to the best achievable. Median 89% of the achievable gain over 180 cases, against 40% for the Taylor rule. It did worse than doing nothing in 5 cases of 180, by at most 0.2 loss points; the Taylor rule did in 28, by much more (lowest share -196%). The search beat the LQ rule in every case.

  2. The weak spot is now the neoclassical economy (LQ median 47%, the same as Taylor’s 48%). With wages that adjust fast, there is little for policy to gain, and the rule’s linearization costs a larger share of it. Three of the five cases worse than no policy are the neoclassical spending boom over 20 rounds.

  3. The old failure at the 0% floor is gone. The neoclassical demand drop over 20 rounds with inflation weighted double was -202% of the achievable gain. Against the new no-policy path and with tuned costs, the lowest LQ share in any case is -9%.

  4. The browser cost is small. The tuned costs for the five presets and every pair of welfare weights are precomputed (tuned_costs.js), so the usual games need no tuning; other settings are tuned at the start of the game. Measured in Chrome, 12 rounds, from pressing Play in round 1 until both scores are ready, with the CPU slowed 6 times (an older phone):

    Game Time
    Two universes, presets (table) 1.1 s
    One universe, preset (table) 0.7 s
    One universe, custom settings (tuned live) 1.7 s

    Tuning alone takes 0.1 to 0.3 s per universe on this laptop.

Recommendation

Unchanged: the search as the optimal path and the “share of the achievable gain” score, and the LQ rule as “Autopilot: optimal rule” and the optimal-move hint. Both now use no policy as defined above, and the rule is judged by the same welfare loss as the class.
