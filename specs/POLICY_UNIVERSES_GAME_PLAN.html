Policy Universes: build plan

Published

October 27, 2026

Plan for building the Policy Universes simulation app. Design note: specs/POLICY_UNIVERSES_GAME.md. This plan covers the app only. How it is used in class and in Canvas is planned separately.

What it is

A stand-alone simulation app that runs on any static server (GitHub Pages, a Canvas iframe, or a laptop with no network after it loads).

  • Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. No build step, no server code, no login.
  • No external libraries loaded at run time. Charts and gauges are drawn in SVG by the app itself, so nothing depends on a CDN.
  • Works on a projector, a laptop and a phone.
  • All numbers are illustrative, and the app says so on screen.

Open decision

Levers. Recommended: one demand lever, “government spending and taxes” (Ch 12 fiscal policy), plus one supply lever, “invest in education and technology.” Monetary policy is Ch 15 and has not been taught yet; it can be added later as a second lever.

The model

The same engine as the Oct 27 slides, ported to JavaScript:

  • Same curves. sras_p and ad_p from slides/neoclassical_slides.qmd:45-46.
  • Same adjustment rule. Each round SRAS closes lambda times the output gap (slides/neoclassical_slides.qmd:316).
  • Engine change needed: potential GDP is fixed at 9,500 in the R code. It becomes a variable so the supply lever and the productivity shock can move LRAS.

Adjustable settings

Every universe setting is a control on a settings panel, set separately for each universe. The defaults below are presets, not fixed values.

Setting Control Keynesian preset Neoclassical preset
Wage and price adjustment (lambda) Slider, 0 to 1.5 0.10 (sticky) 1.0 (flexible)
Spending multiplier on the demand lever Slider, 1 to 4 2 1
Share of policy that is expected Slider, 0 to 1: the share of a policy move that SRAS matches in the same round, so it shows up in the price level instead of output 0 (moves output) 1 (mostly the price level moves)
Policy lag Rounds, 0 to 3 1 1
  • Preset buttons: Keynesian, Neoclassical, and Real world. Real world starts slow and turns flexible after a set number of rounds (the OpenStax 13.1 rule of thumb). It adds two settings: lambda later, and the round it switches.
  • Reset returns a universe to its preset.
  • Shareable settings: the current settings are written into the page address, so a link opens the app with those settings. This needs no server.
  • Settings lock once round 1 starts and unlock on a new game, so a game’s results always match one set of settings.

The rounds

There are 10 rounds by default (adjustable). The same scripted shocks hit both universes:

  • Round 2: demand falls
  • Round 5: oil shock (SRAS shifts left)
  • Round 7: boom
  • Round 9: productivity gain

A “custom” option lets the player choose the shock in each round instead.

Scoring:

  • Each round loses points for unemployment away from 5% and for inflation away from target.
  • Supply investment costs points now and pays off 3 rounds later.
  • Each universe keeps its own score.

The dashboard

One dashboard per universe, side by side, with the same scales on both so they compare at a glance.

Gauges, current period:

  • Real GDP vs potential (the output gap, in percent)
  • Unemployment rate, with the 5% natural rate marked
  • Inflation rate, with the target marked
  • Price level

Tracking over time:

  • An AD/AS panel for the current round, with the past equilibria as a fading trail.
  • Line charts by round for real GDP and potential, unemployment, inflation, and the price level. Both universes are drawn on the same chart so the paths compare directly.
  • A Phillips curve trace: inflation against unemployment, one point per round.
  • A round log: the shock, the policy chosen, and the results in both universes.

Controls: the demand lever, the supply lever, and a “next round” button.

End screen: both scores, lost output, peak inflation, and the full paths.
